The Wikipedia game

You know the game: start on a random Wikipedia page and reach a target by clicking only links in the article text. We played it on our 303 Marvel pages with Spider-Man as the target. The arrows matter here: you can only click links the page actually contains.

It's absurdly easy. 106 pages link straight to him, and anyone who can reach him at all gets there in at most 4 clicks. Getting away from him is a different story: his page links to only 9 characters, so reaching the far corners from Spider-Man takes up to 6 clicks. Try it:

The longest trips. Only three characters need all 4 clicks to reach Spider-Man: Mahr Vehl, Thin Man and Thena. Each is a deep cut whose page links only to other deep cuts. Thena's route runs Makkari → Quasar → Nova → Spider-Man: from the Eternals out through the cosmic heroes. In the other direction, Box (Alpha Flight) and Motormouth are 6 clicks from Spider-Man's page, because you first have to leave his tight circle of nine and cross the universe. And 29 characters can't be reached at all: the 17 loners, the 9-character island from week 1, and three pages (Peggy Carter, Synapse, The Witness) that link to no other Marvel superhero, so there's nowhere to click next.

Try to break the Marvel universe

Which single character holds the universe together? We deleted each of the 277 characters in the giant component in turn and checked who got cut off. The answer is almost nobody. Delete Spider-Man and you lose exactly 5 characters: the five pages whose only link is to him (Enigma, Ethan Edwards, Helix, Phil Grayfield and Thunderclap). Everyone else still has a route around the hole. Black Widow comes second (3), and Doctor Strange third (2).

So one deletion does nothing. What about a series of deletions? Below, three villains delete characters one at a time. Random picks anyone. The Popularity Hunter always deletes the current highest-degree character. The Broker Hunter recomputes betweenness after every deletion and takes out whoever is now the biggest bridge. Drag the slider or press play:

Random deletion is hopeless. After 100 deletions the giant component still holds about 169 characters, only about 8 fewer than the 177 still standing. Both hunters halve the universe in 60–75 deletions, and for the first 50 they are neck and neck: the same hubs top both lists. Then they split. The Popularity Hunter keeps deleting whoever still has the most links, while the Broker Hunter targets the bridges between the X-Men, the cosmic characters, the street-level heroes and the rest. At 80 deletions the Broker Hunter has left a giant piece of only 42 characters; the Popularity Hunter's is still 120. Popularity keeps the universe busy; brokers keep it in one piece.

The real broker is Hercules

Spider-Man has the highest betweenness: 23% of all shortest paths run through him. But is that a finding? We rewired the network 200 times keeping every character's number of links, and asked how much betweenness each character gets just from their degree:

Spider-Man's 23% is exactly what 106 links buy you in any wiring. It's his popularity, not his position. Hercules has twice what his 26 links predict, 4.3 standard deviations above the shuffles. He's an Olympian god who is also an Avenger, a Champion and the Hulk's best friend, so his page joins the gods and cosmic heroes to the Earth-based teams. Black Widow scores just as high, but we checked: most of her surplus comes from being the only link to three obscure characters (Blue Eagle, Rockman and The Witness). Black Cat is the opposite: 28 links but a third of the expected betweenness, because 24 of her 28 neighbours are also Spider-Man's, so every path near her has a shortcut around her.

The X-Men are one giant clique

The biggest groups in which everyone links to everyone have 8 members. There are six of them, and all of them are X-Men. They share a core of six (Betsy Braddock, Cyclops, Emma Frost, Jean Grey, Rachel Summers and Wolverine), and each adds two of Cable, Jubilee, Northstar, Storm and Phoenix. Click through them:

Red: the six who are in all six cliques. Pick a clique to light up its 28 links.

Bonus: comic eras stick together. We also pulled each character's first-appearance year from their Wikipedia infobox. 28% of links join two characters from the same decade, against 17% if you shuffle the years among the characters, a z-score of +8.6 against 1000 shuffles. The Golden-Age heroes (the 1930s–40s Invaders) link to each other almost six times more than chance would give. The 1960s characters (Spider-Man, Hulk, Black Widow) barely do, because everyone links to them.

What surprised us

The biggest hub is also one of the easiest to route around. Spider-Man is on nearly a quarter of all shortest paths, yet deleting him cuts off only five one-link characters. The universe has been written so densely around him that every page he connects has another way through. The characters you really can't lose are the quiet bridges between corners, such as Hercules, and you only see them once you compare against the right null.
