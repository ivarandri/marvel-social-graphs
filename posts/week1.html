What we asked

  • Does our copy of the network match the course's stated numbers?
  • Do in- and out-degree really look that different?
  • Who gets linked to, who links out, and why aren't they the same characters?
  • Who lives outside the giant component — and are they anybody we've heard of?

What we did

The snapshot ships as two tab-separated files with # comment headers. The one trap: 17 characters have no edge in either direction, so building the graph from the edge list alone silently drops them. Add every node first.

import csv, pandas as pd, networkx as nx

nodes = pd.read_csv("week1_nodes.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#", quoting=csv.QUOTE_NONE)
edges = pd.read_csv("week1_edges.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#", names=["source", "target"])

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_nodes_from(nodes.node_id)        # every node FIRST -> the 17 isolates survive
G.add_edges_from(edges.itertuples(index=False))

The two edge counts differ for one reason: 1,784 counts every directed link, and 350 of those are reciprocated (A → B and B → A). Collapse each reciprocated pair to one undirected edge and you remove exactly 350 → 1,784 − 350 = 1,434.

The degree distributions

On log–log axes the in-degree runs down a rough straight line all the way out to Spider-Man at k = 106. The out-degree tail bends and stops near k ≈ 28, nearly four times shorter. Is in-degree a power law? Not from this — n = 303 is small and the fit spans barely 1.5 decades. "Straight-ish on log–log" is a shape reading, not a fitted exponent; the honest tool is the cumulative distribution, a week-2 problem.

The reason the two lists barely overlap is who creates each edge. An in-link is conferred: some other article's editors decided this character was worth mentioning — and the more central a character already is, the more editors reach for them, so in-degree keeps compounding (Spider-Man is linked by a third of the whole category). An out-link is authored: somebody sat down and wrote it into this page, so out-degree is capped by how thorough one article happens to be. Betsy Braddock tops out-degree because her page is a continuity-heavy essay that name-drops half the X-books. In this network, in-degree ≈ fame, out-degree ≈ how chatty your own article is.

The edges of the map

Outside the giant blob there's exactly one other connected piece: a 9-character island — Backhand, Blackthorn, Radian, Scaredycat, Scatterbrain, Shear, Snapdragon, Toxyn and Vyking. Several are from the 1980s series Strikeforce: Morituri. Their articles cite each other but not one links to, or from, a single character in the main 277. They're a closed loop the A-list never noticed. (Find them in the map above with the "island" filter.)

What surprised us

Baymax is an isolate. The inflatable robot from a Disney film that made $650M sits in the roster with degree zero — no Marvel-superhero article links to him, and his own doesn't link back.

The same list holds Miracleman (one of the most litigated rights sagas in comics), Super Rabbit, and Yo-Yo Rodriguez, a hero far better known from TV. The lesson from the course's Part 3 lands hard: the network only knows what the harvesting rule captured. Links from navigation templates were deliberately excluded — defensible — but it's also exactly why Baymax, who probably shares a navbox with half the MCU, comes out with no edges at all.
